# **Chapter 26**

The messenger came galloping through the mud at dawn, his horse heaving steam, his lips cracked from shouting orders across countless camps.

“Message for the medical division of the Eastern Post!”

Halden broke the seal with a thumb. His jaw tightened. “Esdel. Pack your things.”

“Sir?”

“Another post — North Ridge — requested emergency apothecary aid. Their only doctor’s been working nonstop for four days. You’re being transferred. Effective immediately.” He slammed the letter on the table. “Apparently you’re the only one they trust to go. They said, *‘Send the backwater genius.’* If I ever meet that officer, I’ll strangle him for stealing my assistant.”

“I’ll be back soon, sir.”

“You’d better. You’re the only one who knows how to brew those antiseptic herbs without burning my nose off.”

Benn rested a hand on Esdel’s shoulder. “Sleep when you can. Eat even if it tastes like dirt. And try not to get stabbed.”

“No promises.”

“Then at least make it a *small* stab.”

Halden handed him a sealed satchel of dried herbs, disinfectant, bandages, and a single small vial glowing faintly blue. “Emergency stimulant. Don’t use it unless you have to. And if you do, try not to die afterward. You better come back in one piece, boy. I still owe you a proper lecture.”

“I’ll hold you to that.”

An escort waited at the gate, a young soldier, twenty at most, with a calm, tired look. “Name’s Loryn.”

They rode the whole of the day under a sky that turned red at its edges. By dusk they neared the safe-zone border, and Esdel saw it, and his breath caught.

The horizon was alive. Tens of thousands of soldiers clashed in smoke and fire. And above them two colossal beings dominated the field.

One was a Titan, easily twenty stories tall, its flesh pale and stretched over visible tendons, its eyes hollow pits of blue flame. Every step shattered the earth. Opposite it soared the Imperial Dragon, serpentine, its scales shimmering like molten gold.

The Titan swung an arm the size of a fortress. The dragon answered with a wave of flame that scorched the battlefield into glass, friend and foe alike. The Titan stumbled, half its torso burned away, and instead of dying, it fell toward them.

“Esdel, jump!”

The shockwave threw him from the horse. When he stood, his ears rang, the horse was gone, and Loryn was nowhere in sight.

He did not run. Wounded men were screaming nearby, and something inside him, that strange, stubborn spark, refused to let him leave them. He tore open the satchel and pressed herbs into wounds, covered burns, dragged men to cover. “Hold on! I’ve got you!”

The ground trembled. A massive shadow fell across the field. The Titan lurched toward him, burning blue eyes fixed on him.

“Oh gods…”

---

He could feel its eyes, heavy and hateful. His pulse raced; time seemed to thicken. And somewhere in that fear, his body remembered.

*Weeks earlier, in the quiet of the medical post.* Moonlight through the tent flap. Everyone asleep. Only Esdel awake, a scalpel and a bowl of water on the table before him, trying to find what had triggered it. He had cut his palm, held his breath, driven himself to exhaustion, and nothing worked, until one night he blinked, slowly, on purpose, and the world slowed. The candle’s flicker stretched into eternity.

It cost him. Headaches that split his skull. Nausea. Once, an entire morning when he could not remember the name of a single herb on the shelf. But it passed each time. And after weeks he found a lesser state, a Veil Form, where time crawled and reaction came before thought.

Now, beneath the Titan’s shadow, he blinked — slowly, intentionally.

Sound dulled. The world became precise, painfully so. His eyes glimmered faintly red, the pupils narrowed to slits like a fox’s. The Titan’s movements, massive yet readable. The arcs of flaming arrows. His breath steadied.

*Run. Or stay and die.*

Before he could move, a blinding flame scorched the air. The Imperial Dragon plunged from the heavens and breathed an inferno upon the Titan’s back. The Titan wailed, its focus breaking away from him, and the two colossal beings collided in a storm of claw, flame, and shadow.

Esdel shouted to a pale young soldier beside him, “Stay low! If you can, run! I can’t protect you here!” and could do nothing but watch.

The Dragon caught the Titan by the jaw and forced its mouth open. Fire gathered in its maw, brighter, hotter, divine. It unleashed hell straight down the Titan’s throat. Flame burst from the Titan’s eyes and wounds. Its body convulsed, cracking, melting into slag. When it fell, the earth shook like thunder beneath the world.

And in that silence he saw it: a faint figure standing upon the Dragon’s head, steady, unmoved by the storm. A silhouette against the burning sky. For a moment, the silhouette looked back at him.

Then the Dragon and its rider vanished into the clouds, leaving only ruin, heat, and the echo of their fury.

Esdel stood trembling, his vision blurred from the Veil’s strain. The Veil let go of him, and with it went the last of his strength. He sat down in the ash, and found his hand already at his collar, holding the silver flower through his shirt.

*Not tonight,* he thought. *Not here. I promised.*

It was a small thing to say to a chain of silver. It was all the prayer he had.

Somewhere, farther down the road, Loryn was alive. Esdel would find that out later, with a broken arm and a limp, and be more grateful than he knew how to say.
