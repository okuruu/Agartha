# **Chapter 3**

The world drifted in and out of focus.  
Time had lost its shape.

He walked because stopping meant dying, and dying — though tempting — felt unfinished somehow, like closing a book before knowing the ending.

His feet moved without thought, his body an automaton driven by something older than will. The desert stretched around him in endless waves of colorless dust. Above, the sun bled faintly through a veil of sandstorm clouds, its light distorted, unnatural — a red that promised nothing but ruin.

Sometimes, he thought he saw *footprints*.

They appeared just ahead of him — small, delicate, pressed into the sand as though left moments ago. He followed them instinctively, his heart stuttering with a hope he didn’t understand. But each time he stepped forward, the prints vanished, swallowed by the wind as if mocking him.

Still, he chased them.

The air grew heavier. Sound began to change — the wind no longer whistled but *spoke*. Whispers curled through the dunes, forming half-words that dissolved when he tried to listen.

“...you were…”  
“...left behind…”  
“...find her…”

He turned sharply, expecting to see someone behind him, but there was only the horizon, rolling and cruel. The voices faded into laughter — his laughter? — echoing back at him from nowhere.

His mind began to fracture under the heat and the silence.

Images burst unbidden behind his eyes. Screams. The sound of metal clashing. A woman crying his name. A hand reaching out from the fire, her face blurred by light and ash.

He fell to his knees, gripping his head as pain split through his skull.

“Who am I?”  
The words tore out of him like a wound reopening. The desert gave no answer. Only the wind continued its cruel song.

Storms began to form again on the horizon — violent and red, flashing with lightning that carved the sky like a blade. He stumbled forward, vision dimming, each breath a struggle. His body trembled with exhaustion and fever.

The whisper beneath his skin throbbed painfully, spreading warmth through his veins — not healing, but *remembering*. It was as if his blood itself was calling something forgotten, something that shouldn’t wake.

The world around him blurred — sand melting into light, light bending into shadow. He could no longer tell what was real.

And then he saw *her*.

At first, just a silhouette.  
A figure of white standing at the edge of the horizon, unmoving, serene amid the storm.

Her hair drifted with the wind, glinting faintly under the flashes of lightning. She faced away from him, her back turned as if she hadn’t noticed — or didn’t care — that he was there.

He tried to call out, but his voice failed him. Only a rasp escaped his throat.

The sky cracked open above them, pouring red light over the dunes. The storm howled louder, tearing at his clothes, but the woman in white did not move.

He stumbled toward her, one step, two — then collapsed.

The sand rose to meet him.

He caught one last glimpse of her — the faint tilt of her head, the fall of her hair across her shoulder — and then the world folded inward.

Darkness rushed in, absolute and final.

Yet as his vision faded, he thought he heard her speak — a voice soft and distant, threading through the storm.

“You were never meant to forget.”

And then, silence.  
Only the wind remained, sweeping away his footprints as though he had never existed.
