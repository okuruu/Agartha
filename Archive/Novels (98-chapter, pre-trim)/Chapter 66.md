# **Chapter 66**

The wind had not ceased its whisper since dawn. It slid beneath the tent seams like an uninvited ghost, carrying with it the thin, metallic scent of old blood and burned wood. In the distance, the battlefield slept uneasily—its silence was the kind that came not from peace, but from exhaustion, a temporary cease in the endless, grinding conflict.

Within the heart of the command encampment, General Lián Xueyao stood before a war table scarred with years of conflict. The map before her was a tapestry of ambition and loss. She had endured the cost, rising from a mere knight to the rank of General through sheer competence and unyielding will. But three years was an eternity, and the deep, pervasive fatigue of command now claimed her, demanding payment.

In her gloved hand, she held the official **grant of leave**, signed by the Supreme Commander: a three-month respite before she was due back to the fray. It was a lifeline and a timer, a reprieve that felt too short to matter. After three refusals, her fourth petition had been granted without a word of explanation — and that unsettled her more than any refusal had.

Despite the heavy, dark General's uniform she wore—its shoulders wide and rigid—she remained a woman of startling, almost ethereal beauty. Three years of harsh campaigning, sleepless nights, and the cruel northern sun had failed to diminish the **smooth, pale fairness of her skin**. Her figure, though currently concealed beneath the thick, protective steel fittings and the dark wool of her uniform, was exquisitely proportioned, lending her movements a rare, controlled grace that few men on the front possessed. Her **long, dark hair**, was pulled back severely into a soldier’s queue, yet loose strands that escaped framed a face of startling classical beauty: high cheekbones, perfect lips, and **golden eyes** that now mirrored the lamp's light with an almost painful, world-weary sorrow. She was a vision of perfection draped in the brutal tools of war.

She looked up, her gaze settling on **Changli**, her most trusted attendant and friend since childhood. Changli was the same age and possessed a beautiful, gentle elegance that was the perfect counterpoint to Xueyao’s hardened command presence. Changli was dressed in simple, yet exquisitely tailored traveling silks, ready for their journey.

“The orders are confirmed,” Xueyao said, her voice dry and low. “Three months. The Lián Estate is within the Kingdom’s walls. We can make the journey in five days. I have secured **fifteen soldiers** of Lián House from the veteran ranks to form our personal escort. We need a medical staff too.”

Changli stepped closer, her expression shifting to one of keen calculation. “My Lady, for that slot — the same man we spoke of. **Apothecary Esdel.**”

Xueyao raised an elegant eyebrow, her exhaustion momentarily forgotten. “I meant to send him ahead alone. Now that I am going myself — why a field apothecary, Changli? I could summon a physician from the royal academy in the capital.”

Changli’s voice softened slightly, laced with practical cunning. “Precisely, My Lady. This journey, despite the official seal, worries me. And when we are in the capital, we may need a medical mind who is not already loyal to the politics of the Kingdom or the Estate. Esdel is already here, embedded in the Ninth Division.”

She paused, then delivered the final, cold calculus. “More importantly, his term of levied service is nearly spent. He is due for discharge in three months — about when our leave ends. He is a man with no deep roots, no political ties, and is utterly dependent on this letter of deployment for his future records. Should the journey prove hostile, or should his knowledge become… *inconvenient* in the capital, he is **expendable** without repercussion to the House.”

Xueyao stared at the map, running a gloved finger over the marked road to the capital. *Expendable.* The word settled in her gut, tasting like the bitter tang of gunpowder. She was tired of the front, but she knew the capital’s court was merely a different kind of battlefield. The thought of bringing a man whom she could easily **dispose of should things turn sour** held a ruthless appeal. This trip was not a vacation; it was a strategic withdrawal.

“Very well,” Xueyao confirmed. “Apothecary Esdel it is. Summon him. I need his official letter prepared, designating him as medical personnel attached to the General’s private security detail.”

Esdel arrived not long after, summoned from the fever-scented medical tents. He entered the General’s personal chamber with the quiet, professional air of a man used to urgent calls. His uniform bore only the stains of tinctures and blood.

“Apothecary Esdel,” Xueyao said. “You are to accompany me, General Lián Xueyao, to the capital for my period of sanctioned leave. You are designated as medical auxiliary for my private escort of fifteen soldiers. This letter secures your position and officially grants you leave from the Ninth Division.”

Esdel accepted the thick parchment—stamped with the General’s personal seal—with careful hands. It was the official sanction he needed to leave the front without being marked as a deserter. He looked briefly at the letter, then at the General.

“I understand, General,” he said, his voice low and even. “I will gather the necessary supplies immediately. My focus will be on the health and discretion of the unit.”

“Excellent,” Xueyao replied. “Changli will finalize the logistics.”

Esdel bowed precisely—a formal, precise movement—and then turned to leave. He disappeared back toward the medical tents, the official letter clutched in his hand, a soldier obeying the highest command.

---

The air in the outer barracks was cold and dark, smelling only of damp canvas and distant woodsmoke. It was the deepest part of the night, when even the sentries moved with weary silence.

**Changli** found Esdel checking the weight distribution of his medical satchels outside his tent. She approached silently, her silk travel trousers rustling barely audibly.

“Preparing for our journey, Apothecary?” she murmured, her voice a low, melodic purr that was entirely out of place in the desolate camp.

Esdel immediately straightened, his movements economical. “Attendant Changli. Yes. I must ensure the medicinal herbs are properly stored to withstand the five-day journey.”

Changli stopped directly in front of him, close enough that he was forced to inhale the soft, expensive floral perfume that clung to her—a scent that stood in stark contrast to the battlefield stench. The lantern light caught the faint shimmer of her dark, beautiful hair and the graceful curve of her neck. Changli was utterly aware of her own presence: her features were exquisite, her gown intentionally tailored to hint at her **attractive, elegant body**. She had used this quiet power many times to gain influence, information, or favors.

“Such serious focus,” she said, her voice dropping lower, teasing. Her **strikingly beautiful** eyes, dark and knowing, held his gaze with a glimmer of mischief. “The General will expect absolute dedication, of course, but even soldiers need comfort on the long road, wouldn’t you agree?”

Esdel did not flinch, nor did his expression change. The cold, distant reflection of the lamplight in his eyes remained.

“My duty is to ensure the General and the escort remain in peak medical condition,” he replied evenly. “Comfort is a luxury reserved for the victorious, not for those of us in service.”

Changli took a slow step closer, her hand rising to brush lightly against the rough wool of his sleeve. Her seductive smile was wide now, challenging.

“You’re too harsh on yourself, Apothecary. We leave the war behind tonight. Think of the warm beds, the quiet rooms, and the soft company that awaits in the capital. Sometimes,” she leaned in just close enough that her breath feathered his ear, “a small indulgence ensures better service.”

She allowed her body to press subtly against his arm, a direct, undeniable invitation, relying on the primal response her beauty typically elicited.

Esdel remained utterly still. The pressure of her touch, the potent fragrance, and the blatant challenge in her gaze seemed to wash over him without penetration. He didn't pull away, but he didn't lean in either. He was a statue, carved of duty.

“Attendant Changli,” he said, his voice perfectly steady, carrying neither judgment nor temptation. “I am assigned to General Lián Xueyao for medical auxiliary support. My presence is secured by an official order, and my focus is solely on my commission. Any action that risks my reputation or distracts from my designated role is unacceptable.”

He then lifted his satchel, forcing her hand away from his sleeve with the necessary motion of adjusting the strap.

“If you will excuse me,” he finished, his tone utterly professional, “I have inventory logs to finalize before the dawn assembly. Good night, Attendant.”

He bowed slightly—formal, precise—and then turned away, disappearing toward the dark, quiet lines of the auxiliary tents. The crunch of his boots against the packed dirt was the only sound.

Changli watched him go. For a long moment, the teasing, seductive smile that had captivated countless men remained frozen on her beautiful face. Then, slowly, as the realization of his complete lack of reaction sunk in, the smile faded. Her expression went **flat, quiet, and supremely thoughtful**. Her striking beauty and seductive methods had failed entirely. Unmoved.

She murmured softly to the dark air, “Unmoved. A true man of principle, or merely a machine?”

---

A short while later, Changli slipped into General Xueyao’s private quarters. Xueyao was still awake, finishing a final set of complex cipher messages.

“Report, Changli,” the General ordered, not looking up.

“He is steady, My Lady,” Changli said immediately, her tone entirely businesslike, the seductive languor gone. “I delivered the test as requested. He did not succumb to charm, vanity, or temptation. He cited only his duty and his official commission as reason for refusal. He is a man who adheres strictly to the letter of the law.”

Xueyao finally lifted her gaze. Their eyes met—a silent exchange of calculation and understanding.

“He barely noticed your beauty?” Xueyao asked, a faint trace of satisfaction ghosting across her lips.

“He barely noticed me at all, General. Only the proximity to his task. He is exactly what we need: a man of **principle, predictable, and fully controlled by his orders**. He will not betray us for gold or lust. If the situation requires, he will be perfectly manageable.”

A long, significant silence followed. General Lián Xueyao had spent three years trusting her life to soldiers, but rarely trusting their characters. Changli’s test, though risky, provided the ultimate assurance.

“Good,” Xueyao said softly, her golden eyes reflecting a newfound, brittle confidence. “That is why I **trust him** now, Changli. His integrity is based on his own disciplined will, not on his devotion to my rank. Finalize the escort. We leave at the change of the guard.”

And outside, as the first cold signs of dawn began to grey the eastern horizon, General Lián Xueyao and her escort—now including the predictable, expendable, and indispensable Apothecary Esdel—began their journey toward the secured walls of the Kingdom, carrying the scent of war into the promise of a brief, beautiful, and strategically volatile respite.
