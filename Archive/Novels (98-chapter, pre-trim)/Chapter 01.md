# **Chapter 1**

The first thing he felt was *heat*.  
 It burned through his skin like punishment from a god he could no longer name.

When his eyes opened, the sun above him was merciless — an unending white fire that scoured the horizon. Sand stretched in every direction, a golden ocean without mercy, and he was its lone castaway.

He couldn’t remember his name.  
He couldn’t remember *anything*.

Blood — his own — had dried into a dark crust over his chest and arms. His clothes, if they could still be called that, hung in tatters. Each breath was a battle, each step a defiance of something greater than death. He didn’t know if he was running *from* something or *toward* it — only that standing still felt like surrender.

Hours, or perhaps days, passed beneath the burning sky. The world shimmered with heat and silence, until the silence broke. The faint whistle of wind carried something unnatural — a whisper, too deliberate to be the desert’s own voice. He turned, but nothing was there.

And then, in the distance, he saw it — a dark wound carved into the dunes.

A cave.

He staggered toward it, dragging his body through the sand. Inside, the temperature dropped sharply, a relief so sudden it almost made him collapse. The air was old, filled with the scent of rust and forgotten ages.

There were signs of life here once — the remnants of a world long buried.  
Ancient tools, their edges dulled by time.  
A strip of faded cloth, its colors ghostly and thin.  
Someone had lived, and died, here.

Deeper in, he found the impossible.

A pool of water — pristine, untouched, gleaming under the dim light that filtered through cracks in the ceiling. The surface was too still, too perfect, like glass that reflected a world not quite this one.

Thirst overcame reason.  
He drank, then washed the blood and sand from his body. The pain dulled, but his wounds did not heal. The water cooled his flesh, yet left a strange pulse beneath his skin — as if something had woken *inside* him.

When he finally turned to leave… the cave was gone.  
Behind him stretched nothing but unbroken desert.

He stared in disbelief.  
Had he walked in circles? Hallucinated? The air shimmered with heat again, and then — a sound. A low growl, distant but rising, vibrating through the sand.

From the horizon, a shape crawled closer — too large, too fluid to belong to any beast he knew. Its body was wrong, shifting between flesh and shadow, its eyes like molten glass.

He ran.  
It followed.

When it struck, it was silent — no roar, no warning. Claws tore through the air, and pain exploded through his shoulder. He swung back wildly, his hand finding a jagged bone half-buried in the sand, and drove it into the creature’s throat.

It howled — a sound that wasn’t sound at all, more like the world *remembering* pain.

Then, as suddenly as it came, it vanished — dissolving into heat and dust.

He looked down and saw his arm — gone, severed above the elbow. Blood poured freely, staining the sand crimson.

He fell to his knees.

The sky blurred above him, a molten halo. His vision dimmed. Somewhere in the haze, a voice — neither male nor female — echoed faintly in his mind.

“You are not the first to wake… and you will not be the last.”

He collapsed, the desert swallowing his blood and his consciousness alike.  
The last thing he saw before darkness took him was the faint glimmer of light on the horizon — like the reflection of water where none should exist.

And then, silence.
