# **Chapter 2**

He woke to silence again.  
No wind. No sun. Only the weight of his own breath echoing in the hollow between life and death.

The desert was colder now — the kind of cold that gnawed through the bones. His body lay half-buried in the sand, caked with dried blood that wasn’t entirely his own. For a long moment, he didn’t move. He simply breathed, letting the pain remind him that he still existed.

Then the ache came — sharp, deliberate, alive.

He looked to his arm. The wound that should have killed him had closed, but not cleanly. Flesh had sealed over the torn stump, leaving behind a line of thumb-sized, darkened spots beneath the skin. They pulsed faintly, like something trying to *breathe* from beneath.

He touched them once.  
They shuddered in response.

A jolt of pain flared through his body — quick, electric, and gone — leaving a whisper in its wake. It wasn’t sound, yet it filled his head.

A voice without words.  
A memory that wasn’t his.

He recoiled. The desert was too empty to answer him, too silent to explain.

He tried to remember — anything, anyone. A name. A face. But the emptiness inside him was perfect, merciless. He was a hollow vessel, filled with echoes of a life erased.

So he walked.

The dunes shifted endlessly beneath his feet until the land changed — rising into broken stone and shadow. Half-buried pillars jutted from the sand like the ribs of a fallen god. Crumbling arches framed fragments of what once might have been a city.

He stepped among the ruins, the sound of his breathing swallowed by stillness. Ancient murals clung to the walls, their colors faded but their message uncomfortably clear.

One showed a man — tall, bare-chested, marked by strange symbols along his arm. His face was indistinct, but something about the shape of his jaw, the curve of his shoulders — it mirrored *him*.

Beside that man was the creature — the same nightmare that had torn through him in the desert. Its form was twisted and divine, half-beast, half-shadow.   
Yet in the painting, the man did not fight it.  
He stood *with* it.

His chest tightened.

Was that him?  
Had he once been master to the monster that hunted him now?

He stumbled back, dust rising in thin ghosts around his feet. His pulse quickened, echoing with that same low vibration beneath his skin — the whisper returning, more insistent now, like a language his body almost understood.

Night fell swiftly, swallowing the ruins in deep indigo. The wind returned — howling low through the broken stone. He found shelter in what remained of a temple’s archway, its roof half-collapsed, its altar worn to smoothness by centuries of sand.

He curled against the cold, every tremor of his body shaking loose another fragment of fear. His eyelids grew heavy. The world outside the ruin blurred into thunder and sand.

And then — light.

A flash split the horizon. Lightning carved the night open, and for a heartbeat, the world was bright again. In that brightness stood *her* — a woman in white, unmoving, her face shadowed by the storm.

She did not speak. She did not move. But the way the wind bent around her made her presence undeniable.

The light vanished.  
So did she.

He blinked into the dark, his breath unsteady.  
Had she been real? Or just another mirage, born from fever and pain?

The whisper under his skin stirred once more — gentler this time, almost a murmur of reassurance or warning. He couldn’t tell which.

“Find me…”

The voice was softer than the wind, but it burned itself into his mind all the same.

And beneath the storm’s distant rumble, the ruins seemed to exhale — as if the ancient stones themselves remembered his name, even if he did not.
