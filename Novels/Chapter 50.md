# **Chapter 50**

The question hung in the air like mist.  
Formal, almost soft — yet heavy with the kind of weight that does not belong to words alone.

“Who are you?”

Lián Xueyao’s voice carried neither suspicion nor gentleness, but something quieter — a pause between thoughts, a tremor of curiosity wrapped in composure. Her golden eyes caught the lantern light, and for an instant, they gleamed like molten dusk — poised, unreadable, and faintly sorrowful.

Esdel froze.

The world seemed to tilt around that single question. He parted his lips to answer, unsure if he should begin with *Esdel* — the name he had lived by — or something else. Something that could explain why he always stood half in shadow, half in silence.

But just as the first word reached his throat, the fragile moment broke.

“My lady,” a voice interrupted softly.

A maid had appeared at the edge of the lamplight, bowing with the precision of someone well-trained to vanish into the background. “Your private chamber is ready.”

Xueyao’s gaze lingered on Esdel for a moment longer — not quite piercing, not quite distant — a faint glimmer of gold reflecting in the dim light. Then, without a word, she turned away. The hem of her robes brushed the ground like whispered silk as she left.

That single look was neither dismissal nor acknowledgment — it was something suspended in between.

The maid followed without sparing him a glance, her expression as polished and unreadable as porcelain. The two of them disappeared down the lamplit path toward the command tents, leaving behind only the faint scent of jasmine and something colder — the quiet echo of what could not be said.

Esdel stood still, caught in the wake of her silence — as though he were a shadow she had already stepped past.

---

Steam coiled in silver ribbons inside Xueyao’s chamber.

The faint glow of lanterns turned the walls into honeyed gold, though their light trembled — as if reluctant to touch the corners of the room. The bathwater shimmered faintly beneath the surface, disturbed only by the slow movement of her fingers.

She leaned back, letting the warmth swallow her body. Her long dark hair floated around her shoulders like threads of ink, strands clinging to her collarbones. The air smelled faintly of lotus and the smoke of half-burnt candles.

For a time, she said nothing.

Then, her voice slipped into the quiet.

“This war…”

Her breath left her as a sigh, her eyes drifting upward to the wooden ceiling where cracks had begun to form like old veins. “It doesn’t end, does it?”

No answer came, of course — only the soft ripple of water and the distant cry of night insects outside her window.

Her mind wandered back to the day’s wounded — the endless moaning, the trembling hands, the way their blood mixed with the rain. She had long learned to steady her heart, to keep her tone even, but lately it had grown heavier to hold. The weight of command was not just orders and strategies — it was the memory of every pair of eyes that had once looked up at her, pleading for life.

She laughed softly — a hollow, private sound that broke too quickly.

“Why don’t the kings fight each other?” she murmured. “One death instead of thousands. Seems fair.”

The words left her lips like a sin whispered to the dark.

She closed her eyes, submerging her face briefly beneath the water. The warmth muffled everything — the world, her breath, her thoughts. When she rose again, her expression had shifted, the faint humor gone, replaced by that same distant calm she wore before her soldiers.

“Enough,” she muttered, pushing the thought away. “Such foolishness.”

Her fingers trailed over the edge of the tub, dripping water onto the tiled floor. She stared at her reflection in the still surface — a woman with tired eyes, a woman trapped between duty and exhaustion, beauty and burden.

The candle beside her flickered and went out. Darkness closed in, soft and tender.  
And she let it.

---

Far from her chamber, the North Ridge post had quieted into a fragile sort of peace.

Esdel sat beneath one of the old trees near the medical division. The moon hung pale above the horizon, casting faint silver over the earth. His uniform still carried the scent of herbs and smoke. Around him, the ground was littered with discarded bandages, empty flasks, and the soft hum of the night.

He leaned back against the trunk, staring through the branches at the fading constellations. The day’s exhaustion had settled deep into his bones, but sleep would not come easily. Not tonight.

In his hand, he held a small silver necklace. Its chain caught the moonlight — thin, fragile, beautiful.

Lyora’s necklace.

He pressed it tightly between his palms, feeling the cool metal bite into his skin. That pain, small and clean, reminded him he was still here — still breathing while so many others were not.

“She’d hate this place,” he whispered, half-smiling to no one. “Too cold. Too quiet.”

The silence that answered him was kind in its cruelty.

He remembered her laughter — how it used to rise above the sound of rain — and the way she used to fix his collar, her fingers brushing herbs like an afterthought. He remembered her last smile, the one that didn’t reach her eyes.

He swallowed. The lump in his throat stayed.

All he had left now was that necklace, a piece of her warmth sealed in silver. And the memories that refused to fade — no matter how hard he tried to bury them beneath duty and exhaustion.

He curled up where he sat, drawing his knees close, resting his forehead against them. The world blurred at the edges — the grass cool beneath him, the tree above him sighing in the wind.

“Lyora…” he whispered again, softer this time. “I still…”

The words broke apart before they finished.

He closed his eyes. The sound of the night grew distant — cicadas, wind, the faint drip of water from the eaves. All of it melted into one quiet hum.

And slowly, he drifted into sleep — curled beneath the tree, the necklace clutched in his hand, the moon a thin silver ghost above him.

If dreams came for him that night, they carried the scent of her hair, the warmth of her laughter, and the ache of everything left unsaid.
