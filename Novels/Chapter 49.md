# **Chapter 49**

The night was still burning faintly in the distance — streaks of red crawling against the horizon, the scent of smoke clinging to every breath. Esdel sat on a crate beside the road, exhaustion weighing down his limbs. The horse beside him twitched nervously, its flanks slick with sweat.

He looked up as she approached — the woman with the bearing of nobility and a gaze that seemed to pierce the night itself.

“...You’re from the main post,” he murmured, voice hoarse.

The woman halted. Her golden eyes regarded him briefly, their light reflecting the moon’s pallor. “You were on the field,” she said quietly. It wasn’t a question.

Esdel nodded once. “Got thrown in there by mistake. I wasn’t supposed to fight.”

Her gaze dropped to his hands — the faint tremor, the dried blood, the scorched hem of his coat. Then, to his eyes. They were brown again. Ordinary. Human.

But she had seen something a moment before — when he’d looked up under the moonlight. A flicker of red. A narrow slit of impossible light, gone too quickly to doubt.

Her tone cooled. “Your eyes.”

Esdel frowned slightly, confused. “My eyes?”

She took a step closer. Even in the dimness, her presence carried weight — quiet, regal authority. The faint shimmer of gold embroidery traced her figure, and the wind lifted a few strands of her midnight hair.

“Do not play ignorant,” she said. “I saw it. The mark of the **Karunākṣa.**”

The name struck Esdel like a blow. He froze. “You… you mean my eyes?”

For the first time, something shifted in her expression — not surprise, but memory. A flicker of old reminiscence.

“I met them once,” she said softly. “Long ago. The bearers of those eyes.”

She looked away briefly, her gaze turning toward the embers on the horizon. “They were powerful. Too powerful. Their sight saw truth the dragons themselves feared. And so… they were gone. Every last one.”

Esdel’s heartbeat quickened. “Gone? But then—”

“—Then tell me,” she cut him off sharply, her tone like frost. “How do *you* possess their eyes?”

Esdel stared at her, his throat dry. “I… don’t know. It just— it happened. I almost died, and then—”

Her golden eyes narrowed. She stepped closer again, close enough that he could see the faint shimmer of her lashes, the perfection of her posture, the unflinching pride in her bearing.

“Enough,” she said. “Do not insult me with vague excuses.”

Her voice dropped, calm yet commanding — a tone that could silence generals.

“You carry something that should not exist. You bear the sight of the dead. The *House of Kālākṣa* was eradicated for a reason — for what they could see, for what they could *become.*”

Esdel’s hands curled into fists. “Then tell me what they were.”

Her lips pressed into a thin line. “No.”

The single word carried the weight of finality.

“I owe you no answer. But you…” She paused, her gaze hardening. “You will answer me.”

The wind stirred between them, carrying the faint sound of the dragon’s distant roar.

Her golden eyes locked onto his. “Who are you?”

The silence stretched — long, breathless, heavy with suspicion and unspoken truths.

And then, before he could speak, the night thundered again with the echo of something vast — far off, yet stirring.

The Imperial Dragon’s voice rumbled faintly in her mind, restless, hungry.  
She clenched her jaw, eyes still on Esdel.

Neither of them moved.
