# **Chapter 4**

He awoke to the sound of *water*.

Not the howling of the wind, not the hiss of sand, but the murmur of a stream trickling somewhere nearby — gentle, constant, impossibly real. His eyelids fluttered. Light pressed against them, soft and golden. The air was cool, heavy with the scent of grass and earth.

When he breathed, it hurt — not with the agony of the desert’s heat, but the ache of a body remembering it had survived.

He did not know that, days earlier, a passing caravan had found him lying near the desert’s edge — a broken figure half-buried in crimson sand. They thought him dead at first. His skin was pale, his pulse faint, his body limp as though the life had long since left it. But one of the travelers, a woman with calloused hands and eyes sharp as glass, had felt the faintest tremor under his ribs.

“He breathes,” she had whispered. “Barely.”

So they had carried him with them — through nights of sandstorm and days of blistering light — until at last, the dunes gave way to green.

The village that welcomed them was a quiet miracle.

Nestled between low hills and silver streams, it shimmered like an oasis untouched by time. Wildflowers clung to the riverbanks. Tall trees arched above narrow paths, their leaves whispering secrets with every gust of wind. The air smelled of rain and herbs, and the distant chime of bells marked the passing of hours that no longer belonged to the desert’s cruel rhythm.

Here, people *laughed*.

Children chased each other along the stream. An old man carved small wooden charms under the shade of a willow. Women hung herbs from their porches, their laughter mingling with the hum of bees. Life moved slowly, peacefully — as if the world beyond these forests had long ceased to exist.

When the caravan arrived, the villagers gathered around the strange, unconscious man they carried. His clothes were scorched, his skin marred by wounds that defied reason. Yet beneath the dirt and blood, something about him felt *ancient*.

They laid him in the care of the local apothecary — a humble building draped in ivy, its walls lined with bottles that caught the sunlight like gems. The scent of crushed mint and dried lavender filled the air.

The apothecary herself worked quietly, her hands practiced and calm. She cleaned his wounds, changed his bandages, whispered small prayers under her breath. The villagers spoke of him in hushed tones — a wanderer, perhaps cursed, perhaps chosen — but none dared disturb the stillness that seemed to surround him.

Days passed.  
His body remained still. His chest rose and fell like the faint pulse of dying embers.

Outside, the village thrived. The river sang. The forest breathed. It was a world alive with color and sound — a world that *should* have felt like peace.

But to those who lingered near his bedside, there was something else.  
At night, when the lamps burned low, they swore they saw faint light beneath his skin — a pulse like liquid fire running through his veins, too slow to be natural, too alive to be death.

The apothecary, watching over him in silence, reached out once — fingertips brushing his wrist — and felt the faintest tremor.

It wasn’t a heartbeat.  
It was something older, deeper.  
Something that did not belong to this place.

Outside, the forest whispered in its sleep. The moon rose, washing the leaves in silver. The village was peaceful — almost sacred.

And within that peace, the man dreamed — though not of the desert, nor of the woman in white, but of something far beneath them both.

A whisper waiting to wake.
