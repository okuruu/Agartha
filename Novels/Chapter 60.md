# **Chapter 60**

Two months had passed since the docks drowned in rumor and blood.

Two months since Lord Lián Hengzhi’s name was blackened, his power stripped bare, and ten banners of the House of Reng were ordered to fall beneath the blade—a calculated sacrifice to prove the loyalty of the one who remained. 

The capital had already moved on — merchants gossiping of rising grain prices near the city gates, courtiers whispering of the severity of the coming winter in the protected halls of the palace. But in the throne hall, beneath the tall arches of carved river stone and hammered gold, memory lingered not as a fading echo, but as smoke that refused to dissipate, clinging to the heavy velvet draperies and the very air the King breathed. Aldric Reng entered the chamber alone.

His gait was measured, a carefully calibrated movement, every step echoing across the expanse of polished black marble that stretched from the doors to the dais. His cane clicked faintly — a subtle, rhythmic counterpoint to the absolute silence of the court. It was a mark of the wound he’d “sustained” during the dockside attack, a phantom injury that now served a greater, theatrical purpose than it ever caused him pain. 

The scar along the back of his right hand had healed pale and thin, a flawless white line against his skin, a perfect, unassuming accessory to his performance of unwavering fidelity and recent hardship.

He paused just before the foot of the dais, bowing so deeply that the rich velvet of his surcoat brushed the cold stone. The King sat in still silence high above him — robed in the solemn colors of the imperial household, deep indigo and burnished silver. His gaze was steady yet distant, fixed somewhere beyond Aldric, like a man watching waves crash upon a desolate shore he knew.

Aldric straightened, holding his breath for a precise beat, letting the air thicken before he spoke. His voice was smooth as aged silk, carrying easily through the vast, empty space.

“Your Majesty.” The title fell softly but carried a deliberate, almost monumental weight. He lingered in the silence, waiting until the faint rustle of the court’s unused draperies had died completely, until the faint echo of his own word was swallowed by the stone.

“Though my body was broken in the service of the Crown,” he continued, his tone pitched just above a whisper, "my spirit was not. Even now, as ten members of my own kin, ten banners of my house, await your righteous judgment, I offer my blood, my loyalty, and my last breath to the Crown, without hesitation or regret.” The King said nothing, the only response being the barely perceptible shift of shadow along the planes of his face.

Aldric’s eyes flickered briefly upward — not in an act of challenge, which would have been fatal, but in a precise calculation of the King’s reaction. The King’s silence was its own form of pressure, a test. Aldric adjusted his mask of humility. His tone softened further, a veneer of regret draped meticulously over the cold, calculated steel beneath.

“My only regret, Your Majesty, is that I did not serve more swiftly, that I allowed such profound corruption to infect even the branches of my own house. That I housed and nourished the vipers who would strike at the heart of the realm. It is a wound I shall carry until my dying day, far deeper than any that pierced my flesh.” The King’s gaze shifted slightly, finally focusing on Aldric’s face — not enough to speak, but enough for Aldric to feel the absolute, penetrating gravity of it, the suspicion that had begun to rot the foundation of the throne.

“Still,” Aldric continued, using that sliver of acknowledgment to his advantage, stepping forward just enough to let the ambient light of the high torches catch the pale scar on his hand, displaying the visual evidence of his service, “my remaining men have been faithful to their renewed duty. I trust Your Majesty has received their reports — the meticulously intercepted missives detailing the Lián conspiracy, the precise weapon ledgers confiscated from the traitorous branches, and the chilling map of ambush sites clearly marked with the Lián insignia.” He paused, the names and evidence hanging in the air like heavy chains. He spoke as though merely reminding the sovereign of details, though every carefully placed word was meant to seed influence and secure his narrative in the King's mind.

“I did not act from ambition, Your Majesty, nor for revenge against a personal enemy. Only loyalty. Loyalty that demands immediate, decisive action even when it brings misfortune, humiliation, and destruction to one’s own blood.” He let the enormity of the sacrifice settle. “This, I believe, speaks louder than any petition.” The King’s fingers, pale and thin, drummed once on the jade armrest — a faint sound, almost imperceptible, but a clear sign that the silence was being broken.

Aldric inclined his head lower, accepting the subtle tension. “If it would serve the realm, I would gladly offer my life again — and again, and again, until no single thread of treachery remains within our borders, or within my own heart.” The chamber was so utterly still that the faint, sputtering hiss of the torches burning high in their wall mounts could be heard. The great golden banners above swayed almost imperceptibly from the draft created by the high ceiling, the embroidered golden threads glinting like scattered light against the dimness.

The King finally spoke — his voice deep, quiet, but edged with an ancient, resonant power that carried farther than mere sound. It was the voice of a man who had seen too much cunning to be easily swayed.

“Loyalty that requires blood, Lord Reng, is still blood.” Aldric held the profound weight of the statement, his faint, controlled smile utterly unbroken. He met the King’s gaze, offering only humility.

“Then let mine flow first, Your Majesty. It is a price I will pay a thousand times over.” A long, agonizing silence followed, filled only by the slow beating of Aldric's heart, which he willed to remain perfectly steady. Every muscle in his body remained relaxed, yet poised. He was a sword waiting to be sheathed or swung. Neither man moved. Neither looked away from the other’s profound scrutiny. Finally, the King leaned back against the high, solemn arch of his seat — his expression now completely unreadable, a mask of sheer exhaustion and supreme authority.

“You serve well, Aldric Reng,” he said, his tone flat, almost weary, the words lacking any warmth of praise. “Perhaps too well. The House of Reng has shown itself equally capable of producing great loyalists and great threats.” Aldric’s subtle smile deepened by the smallest, most secret degree, though he quickly masked it with a respectful, immediate bow. He had earned his reprieve.

“Too well, Your Majesty, is still not enough for a devoted servant of the Crown.” The King waved a languid hand slightly — not a full dismissal, but a clear signal that his patience for rhetoric and high drama was thinning rapidly.

“Enough. The court is weary of death and devotion alike. Take your rest, Lord Reng.” Aldric lowered his head in perfect submission. “As Your Majesty commands.” He stepped back, the controlled sound of his boots brushing against the stone floor, the faintest rasp that echoed once and was instantly absorbed by the vast, echoing silence of the chamber. The King’s heavy gaze followed him, tracking his every step — a silent, unspoken threat held in reserve.

At the threshold, where the cool shadow of the corridor met the torchlight of the hall, Aldric paused, turned just his head, and bowed once more. His voice was low, measured, and perfectly composed, a final, poisoned dart of self-justification.

“I ask no pardon for what has been done, Your Majesty. Only that the Crown remembers... that the House of Reng has never once turned away from its true, central duty. Others may claim loyalty; the House of Reng bleeds for it.” The King gave no reply. The silence was his answer, a confirmation of the heavy, unsettled debt Aldric now carried.

He simply lifted his hand a fraction higher — a final gesture of dismissal, or perhaps the grudging recognition of a life spared. Aldric bowed deeper, his cane tapping once in a soft, final note of gratitude upon the marble, then turned and departed through the long, deepening corridor. His shadow stretched and fled before him. Behind him, the King remained seated and silent — the faint, slow creak of his throne as he shifted his immense weight the only sign of life in the vast, echoing hall.

And as Aldric’s steps faded into the marble, one thought burned beneath his calm, cool exterior, sharper than any knife and sweeter than any victory — a silent oath disguised beneath the practiced sweetness of his smile: *You will remember me, Your Majesty. And you will be dependent on me.* The torches flickered high on the walls, casting dancing shapes of judgment onto the imperial standards.

The court remained still, suspended in the aftermath of a ruthless political transaction.

And outside, clouds gathered above the capital, low and heavy, smothering the light — the kind that promised both a long, chilling rain and the eventual, inevitable retribution that always follows spilled blood.
