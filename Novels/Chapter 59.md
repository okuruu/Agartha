# **Chapter 59**

The bells of the western harbor had not yet stopped ringing when the rumors reached the palace.

By dusk, the capital was aflame with whispers. *“Lord Lián Hengzhi, caught trading arms to the rebels.”* *“Aldric Reng, the loyal hound of the Crown, bled defending the realm.”* *“The King has ordered a tribunal.”*

Each retelling sharpened the lie until it gleamed like truth.

In the grand hall of the palace, torchlight flickered across gold and marble. The air smelled faintly of iron and candle smoke — the residue of too many sleepless councils. Behind the veiled windows, the last of the sun bled into the horizon, staining the clouds the color of old wine.

The throne room was quiet save for the slow, rhythmic tap of boot heels — the sound of **Grand Duke Rowan Hale** walking across the inlaid floor. His cloak, dark as the sea at night, trailed behind him in perfect silence.

Before him sat **His Majesty**, the King — cloaked in shadow, one hand resting against his temple. The lines around his eyes had deepened since the war began. He did not look up immediately when Rowan bowed low.

“Your Majesty,” Rowan began, his voice smooth, reverent — too polished to sound sincere. “I have come bearing grim tidings from the western docks.”

The King did not answer, only motioned faintly for him to continue.

Rowan straightened, clasping his hands. “The rumors you’ve heard, I fear, are true. Treason has taken root where we least expected it. Lord Lián Hengzhi’s ship — laden with arms and forged letters — was uncovered this morning. The nobles present can testify.”

He paused, letting the words hang in the air like smoke.

“I wish I could tell Your Majesty that it was all a misunderstanding,” Rowan went on softly, “but the evidence was… overwhelming. Even now, the harbor guards have seized the ship. Aldric Reng himself led the investigation — at great personal cost.”

At that, the King’s gaze lifted — faint, questioning.

Rowan inclined his head, as though reluctant to say more. “During the inspection, when the treasonous crates were discovered, several of Lord Hengzhi’s retainers turned violent. They accused Aldric of framing their lord and drew blades against the Crown’s envoy. Aldric was struck in the shoulder — bled badly — but he subdued the rebels and prevented greater loss of life.”

He sighed. “It was chaos, Your Majesty. The docks burned with accusation and fear. By the time order was restored, the people already believed what they saw.”

The King’s voice, when it came, was low — steady, but tired.  
 “Belief,” he said, “is not proof.”

“Indeed,” Rowan replied, bowing again. “And yet belief shapes the realm faster than law. By now, the name of Lián is on every tongue. The nobles are calling for swift justice — not for vengeance, but for stability. The people too. They speak of Aldric’s courage, of his loyalty. He has become a symbol, Your Majesty. A flame that reassures them the Crown still stands unshaken.”

He stepped closer, lowering his tone, as if confiding something sacred.  
 “I do not wish to rush judgment. But the longer the court hesitates, the more the realm trembles. The war has thinned our coffers and our trust. We cannot afford unrest. The Lián name must be silenced — for peace, if nothing else.”

For a moment, the only sound was the distant toll of the evening bell — slow, hollow, final.

The King leaned back, his face half hidden in shadow. His fingers traced the carved armrest absently. Beneath the weariness in his eyes, there was sorrow — the kind that comes from knowing justice may have already been lost, but duty demands obedience anyway.

“Very well,” he said quietly. “For the safety of the realm.”

He reached for the ink brush beside him and signed the order — arrest, tribunal, the seizure of every Lián ship in harbor. His hand trembled only once.

Rowan bowed deeply, his expression unreadable. “You have done what must be done, Your Majesty. History will remember your mercy.”

But as he turned to leave, his lips curved — a small, precise smile that never reached his eyes.

Outside, thunder rumbled faintly beyond the palace walls.

When the echo faded, the King remained seated — alone. His gaze lingered on the ink-stained order before him, and for the first time in years, he looked old.

The candle flame wavered, guttered.

And somewhere in the city below, the first banners calling for Lián House’s downfall were already being raised.
