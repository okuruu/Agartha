# **Chapter 24**

That night, after the Festival of Wishing Threads had ended, the apothecary was quiet. Too quiet.

The laughter, the ribbons, the flicker of lanterns—all had dissolved into stillness. Sleep came for him not as rest but as surrender. His body was heavy, his thoughts a slow tide of color and sound—Lyora’s laugh, Reyn’s words, the fluttering of two ribbons twisting, breaking apart.

Then the dark took him.

---

He stands beneath a sky that is neither night nor day.  
The world breathes in shades of dying gold.

A vast field stretches endlessly ahead, the grass moving like waves in the wind. The air carries the taste of iron and salt—sharp, metallic, alive. The sun bleeds orange on the horizon, drowning behind the edge of the world.

And there—  
a figure.

A young man stands before him, clad in silver armor veined with faint threads of blue light. His hair clings to his forehead with sweat, his breathing ragged. Cracks split his armor like scars, blood trickling through them, dark and glistening.

Esdel doesn’t recognize him.  
And yet, something deep within him twists with terrible familiarity.

The man’s lips move—desperate, urgent. He’s shouting something, maybe a name, maybe a warning. But no sound reaches Esdel’s ears. The world has stolen their voices.

Behind Esdel, a cliff yawns open to a raging sea. The waves crash against the rocks below, white spray leaping into the air. The sound should be deafening, but even that comes muted, as though the world itself holds its breath.

From the forest beyond comes a roar—low, deep, and inhuman. A sound that makes the earth tremble.

The man before him steps closer, eyes wide with fear and fury. His mouth forms words Esdel cannot hear—pleading, angry, then sorrowful. He grabs Esdel’s shoulder, shaking him as if to tear sense into him. Esdel wants to answer, to ask who he is, what this is—but his voice fails him. Nothing comes out.

And then, the man’s expression breaks.

All the fury drains away, replaced by something infinitely worse—resignation. He smiles, barely, a tremor of grief curving his lips. His hand tightens once more on Esdel’s shoulder.

He mouths a single word.  
At first, it’s silent. Then it echoes—softly at first, then thunderously, splitting the still air.

**“Fight.”**

And he shoves him backward.

Esdel stumbles—then falls. The world tilts violently. Wind screams in his ears as the cliff edge vanishes beneath him. The roar from the forest crescendos, breaking the silence like glass.

The world collapses into light.

---

He wakes with a strangled gasp.

The ceiling swims above him in blurred streaks of light and shadow. His lungs burn; his body trembles. Sweat clings cold to his skin, his hair plastered to his forehead.

“Esdel!”

The voice comes from somewhere close—gentle, shaken. His name sounds different in her voice, like something fragile being held too tightly.

The lantern flickers on the bedside table, casting gold across two silhouettes. Arwen stands by the door, a robe hastily thrown over her nightclothes, worry carved deep into her expression. Beside her, Lyora kneels at his bedside, a damp cloth clutched in trembling hands. It smells faintly of lavender water, the kind they keep for fevers. Her green eyes are wide, wet with unshed tears.

“You were screaming,” she whispers, voice breaking. “You weren’t breathing right—I tried to wake you—”

He drags a shaking hand over his face, his breath still uneven. “I… saw something,” he murmurs hoarsely. “Someone.”

Arwen sits down carefully on the edge of the bed, the old wood creaking beneath her weight. Lyora stays close, her hand resting on the blanket near his arm—close enough for warmth to pass between them, but not touching. Not yet.

“Tell us,” Arwen says softly. “If you can.”

Esdel hesitates. His voice trembles when he begins to speak. He tells them everything—the twilight field, the cliff, the silver armor, the roar, the word. His words falter halfway through, and his eyes glaze as though he’s watching it unfold again.

When he finishes, the room is silent. The only sound is the faint hiss of the lantern.

Arwen’s gaze softens, shadowed with thought. “Dreams that vivid often carry a shadow of memory,” she says gently. “Perhaps… something from before you came to us.”

Lyora swallows hard. Her voice wavers. “That must have been terrifying.” She hesitates, then adds in a whisper, “You don’t have to face that alone.”

Esdel turns toward her. Her eyes shine in the dim light—green and glimmering, like wet leaves after rain. Something in him cracks open at the sight. He’s been holding himself together for so long—too long.

“I didn’t think…” His words stumble out, barely a whisper. “…that I’d cry.”

But he does.

It comes quietly, without warning. A tremor first, then tears—silent, unrestrained, slipping down his cheeks as his shoulders shake. He presses a hand against his eyes as if ashamed, but the tears keep coming.

Lyora reaches out, her fingers brushing his wrist, then sliding over his hand until she can hold it properly. Her touch is soft, hesitant.

Arwen moves closer and gathers them both into her arms—steady, grounding, motherly. Lyora leans in without thinking, wrapping her arms around them. Between their warmth, Esdel finally lets go.

No sound leaves him—only the faint tremor of breath, the broken rhythm of grief that has no name.

They stay that way for a long while. The lantern burns low, painting the room in dim, golden light. Outside, the village sleeps.

Inside, the silence is no longer empty.

When Esdel’s tears finally slow, he finds his voice again, rough and small. “It felt so real,” he murmurs. “Like I’d lost something before I even knew what it was.”

Lyora’s thumb brushes the back of his hand. “Maybe you didn’t lose it,” she whispers. “Maybe you just haven’t found it again.”

He looks at her then—and it hurts, how much he feels in that single glance. Gratitude, sorrow, longing. The impossible wish that the distance between them could vanish if he just reached out.

But he doesn’t.

He only nods, his throat too tight for words.

When the lantern finally flickers out, the room falls into gentle darkness. Arwen lingers a moment longer, then leaves quietly, closing the door behind her.

Lyora stays. She wrings the damp cloth once more, placing it across his forehead. “Try to rest,” she says softly.

He closes his eyes, though sleep no longer feels kind.

Her footsteps move away—but just before she reaches the door, she stops. He hears the faint catch of her breath.

And then, barely above a whisper—  
“I was scared,” she says. “When I heard you scream. I thought… I’d lose you, too.”

He doesn’t answer. He can’t. The words strike somewhere deep and raw.

When the door closes, the darkness swells. The night feels heavier, thicker, alive with the ache of what was almost said.

Esdel turns his head toward the window, where the last traces of moonlight spill across the floor.

He watches the faint shimmer on the wooden boards—the pale reflection of two threads tangled and undone.

And in the silence that follows, he finally understands what it means to remember pain—  
and to long for something just out of reach.
