# **Chapter 5**

The first thing Esdel felt was *light.*

It pressed softly against his eyelids, warm and golden — the kind of light that didn’t burn, but healed. He breathed in, and the air tasted of earth and herbs, damp wood and sunlight. Somewhere nearby, water trickled gently, and a quiet hum — a woman’s hum — wove through the air like a lullaby.

He blinked, his eyes adjusting to the pale ceiling above him. Wooden beams stretched overhead, woven with dried vines and hanging herbs. A faint breeze drifted through the open window, carrying with it the sound of leaves rustling and birds singing outside.

For the first time in what felt like eternity, the world was *still.*

He turned his head — and froze.

His *arm.*

Whole. Real.

The memory of the creature’s bite, the unbearable pain, the heat of blood pouring into the sand — it all came rushing back like a fever. He stared in disbelief, flexing his fingers. The flesh looked unbroken, perfectly normal, save for a faint pattern of dark, thumb-sized marks running from his elbow to his wrist. They pulsed once — faintly, rhythmically — before fading back into stillness.

His stomach twisted. “What…?”

A voice answered from the corner of the room, calm and steady.

“Ah. You’re awake.”

An elderly woman stepped into the light. Her silver hair was braided simply, her hands folded in front of her apron. Her eyes — soft green, touched with wisdom and patience — studied him with quiet care.

“You’ve been asleep for three days,” she said. “The caravan that found you left you here in our care. You’re safe now, child.”

Esdel’s voice came rough, uncertain. “My… arm. It was gone. I saw it— I *felt* it gone.”

The woman tilted her head slightly, her expression calm but puzzled. “Gone? No, no. When you arrived, your body was whole. Weak and feverish, yes, but no wounds of that kind. Only exhaustion and starvation.”

“That can’t be right,” he whispered, clutching the arm as if it might tear itself away again. “I remember losing it. The pain… the blood…”

The woman’s expression softened. “Sometimes, when the mind is pushed past its limits, it weaves dreams so vivid they seem real. Perhaps the desert played tricks on you.”

Esdel looked away, the words echoing hollowly in his head. *Dreams.* But the memory of that agony, the tearing sound — it hadn’t felt like a dream.

Before he could speak again, another voice entered — lighter, curious, full of life.

“Grandmother, is he finally awake?”

A young woman stepped in, her arms full of freshly picked herbs. Her hair was dark brown and loose, tied back with a simple cloth. The sunlight caught the green of her eyes, bright as spring leaves.

She smiled at him, soft and relieved. “You had us worried,” she said. “I thought you might never wake.”

The older woman gave a small chuckle. “This is my granddaughter, Lyora Ardynne Aurelisse. I’m Arwen Lysandra Aurelisse.”

Esdel looked between them — the calm, weathered grace of the grandmother and the warm, curious glow of the granddaughter — and for a moment, he forgot his fear.

“Esdel,” he murmured. “My name is Esdel.” The name rose from some deep well inside him — the only fragment of truth that hadn’t slipped away.

Lyora repeated it softly, as though testing its sound. “Esdel… That’s a strong name.”

He gave a faint, tired smile. “It’s all I remember.”

Arwen nodded gently. “Then it’s a good start. You’re in the village of **Edenridge**, Esdel — a small place, tucked at the edge of the forest. You can rest here until your strength returns.”

He glanced toward the open window. Outside, sunlight spilled across cobblestone paths. Villagers passed by, their laughter mingling with the sound of flowing water. A dog barked somewhere near the river. It felt… unreal, after so much death and sand.

“This place,” he murmured. “It’s too peaceful.”

Arwen smiled faintly. “Peace is a fragile thing. You should hold on to it while you can.”

Lyora began arranging herbs in small clay jars, the air filling with scents of mint and rosemary. “You can stay here as long as you need,” she said, not looking up. “Grandmother doesn’t turn anyone away. Not even men who wander in from the desert claiming they lost an arm.”

Her teasing tone made him almost laugh — *almost.*

For the first time in what felt like lifetimes, Esdel’s heartbeat slowed. The tension in his body began to ease, and the world, for the briefest moment, felt kind.

But as he let his head sink back against the pillow, his gaze drifted to his arm again.

The dark spots beneath his skin shimmered faintly, pulsing once before fading.

Neither Arwen nor Lyora noticed.

And in the quiet hum of the apothecary’s clinic, beneath the smell of herbs and sunlit peace, something within Esdel whispered faintly — not in words, but in memory.

“You were never meant to heal.”
