# **Chapter 55**

The candlelight trembled against the polished wood of the study, its glow chasing the edges of darkness that clung to the curtains and corners. The rain outside had not ceased since dusk—soft, deliberate, and persistent, as if the heavens themselves wished to eavesdrop.

**Aldric Reng** stood by the tall window, his reflection fractured by the glass veined with raindrops. His once-proud figure was worn now—his shoulders stooped, his eyes hollowed by sleepless nights and the taste of disgrace that never left his tongue. 

He tightened his gloved hands, fingers twitching as if gripping something invisible, something that used to be his—his pride, his honor, his name.

The candle guttered.

And with it, the last memory of applause and luxury in his mind flickered out — the banquets, the gold embroidery, the laughter in halls where his name once carried weight. All replaced by whispers, smirks, and pitying stares.

He could still hear the king’s decree ringing in his skull.

*“Ten of your bloodline shall answer for your theft. The price of one man’s greed is the shame of his House.”*

Ten names struck like thunder.

Brothers. Cousins. Uncles. All of them to hang.

And yet, Aldric was spared. Not because he was innocent, but because he was useful — a man the king wanted to keep alive long enough to humiliate with survival. The irony twisted like a knife in his ribs.

Behind him, the door opened with a low groan.

“Still awake, Lord Reng?” came a smooth, cool voice.

Aldric turned. **Grand Duke Rowan Hale** entered with the unhurried grace of a man used to being obeyed. His cloak, still damp from the rain, fell like a shadow upon the floor. He did not bother removing his gloves. He didn’t need to. Everything about him—the calculated steps, the deliberate silence—spoke of a predator who knew his prey would not flee.

He shut the door behind him. The sound was soft, but final.

Rowan sat down without invitation, his movements elegant, his gaze sharp as a blade behind calm eyes. “I received your letter,” he said, steepling his fingers. “A desperate one, at that.”

Aldric’s lips twitched. “Desperation sharpens clarity.”

“Mm.” Rowan’s eyes flicked toward him with mild amusement. “And yet, desperation also breeds recklessness. You must know that coming to me—your brother-in-law, of all people—will raise suspicion.”

“I don’t care about suspicion,” Aldric replied, his tone low, restrained only by pride. “I care about survival. About reclaiming what was stolen from me.”

Rowan smiled faintly, as if amused by the rawness of Aldric’s tone. “And what do you propose, then? Speak plainly. You’ve already begged for my protection—surely you didn’t summon me here only to reminisce about your losses.”

Aldric moved closer to the table, his boots clicking against the marble. “Protection, yes. But not charity.” His hand brushed across the stack of parchments he’d prepared—ledgers, maps, contracts, each marked with the sigil of **House Lián**. “You’ll have your compensation. Eight-tenths of Lián’s ports and trade rights. Full control over their eastern commerce routes. And in return, you’ll provide me what I need most—political rehabilitation.”

Rowan’s brows lifted slightly, though not in surprise. “Eight-tenths,” he murmured, tracing the air as if weighing the offer. “That’s... quite generous. Even for a desperate man.”

“I know what power costs,” Aldric said, meeting his gaze. “And I know you don’t invest in loyalty—you invest in leverage.”

The Grand Duke chuckled, low and silken. “You do know me well, Lord Reng. Perhaps too well.”

He leaned back in his chair, letting the silence hang heavy for a moment. Then, with the air of a man savoring the taste of a fine wine, he said, “Tell me, Aldric... what makes **House Lián** your chosen prey? Why them, out of all the houses in this kingdom?”

The question floated like smoke between them.

Aldric’s expression didn’t waver. His eyes—once proud, now cold with calculation—met Rowan’s with unnerving steadiness. The corner of his mouth curved upward, a smile that was neither joy nor mockery.

“Because,” he said softly, “this plan will be perfect.”

Rowan tilted his head slightly, his interest piqued. “Perfect?”

Aldric’s smile deepened, though no warmth reached his eyes. He turned back toward the window, his reflection swallowed by the rain-slick glass.

“You will understand,” he murmured, voice almost lost to the sound of thunder, “as I uncover my motives.”

And with that, the candle flickered once, then died, leaving only the echo of the storm—and two shadows seated in the dark, smiling for entirely different reasons.
