## 1. Writing Brief (the original bible)

Write the next chapter or scene of my novel set in the village of **Edenridge**, a lush, green, laughter-filled, hardworking village with people of warm hearts. It often feels alive with unseen history.

**Writing Style:** Use *Lyrical Melancholy Realism*: prose that is poetic but restrained, sensory yet minimal, emotional yet quiet. Write with atmospheric detail, cinematic pacing, and rhythmic sentences that breathe between moments. Show emotion through small gestures, silences, and sensory cues rather than exposition. Each line should feel deliberate: tender, slow-burning, and quietly introspective.

**Tone:** Maintain a balance of **angst, reflection, and subdued warmth**, a slow-burn emotional current filled with longing, regret, and flickers of hope. Allow soft, natural humor to occasionally cut through the heaviness, but never break the overall stillness.

**Focus Character:** **Esdel**, a capable yet conflicted man torn between duty and emotion, who has lost his memory and lived on the second floor (the attic) of the apothecary. Despite his amnesia, he remains gentle and kind-hearted, often helping others while quietly struggling to understand who he is.

**Core Relationship:** Esdel's unspoken bond with **Lyora Ardynne Aurelisse**, the green-eyed, dark-haired apothecary whose quiet strength and hidden sorrow both ground and unsettle him. Let their connection unfold through silence, glances, unfinished words, and shared moments neither dares to name.

**Narrative Approach:** Introspective narration and sensory-rich imagery (sound, texture, scent, light). Dialogue emotionally charged but restrained, heavy with what's left unsaid. Pacing flows like breath: stillness, tension, gentle release.

**Thematic Core:** Healing through labor. The dignity of restraint. The quiet ache of memory and the longing for connection.

**Ending Tone:** Bittersweet closure or quiet revelation, leaving the reader suspended between loss and hope.

### Style notes learned from the manuscript
- The strongest chapters (5–16, 24, 25–29, 34–38) use short paragraphs, one-line beats, and scent/light/sound cues. Match these. Ch25–29 (*The Unspoken Summer*) is the reference for triangle angst told through gesture.
- Chapters 17 and 57–64 drift into long, ornate sentences with **bold words mid-prose**. Avoid bold in prose; use *italics* only for thoughts, whispers, and emphasis.
- Humor chapters (26, 30, 31, 46, 47) work because the humor is character-driven. Keep it gentle.
- Chapter 24 (the dream) is written in **present tense** on purpose. Dreams and visions may use present tense; everything else is past tense.
- Esdel's "modern" vocabulary (physics, gravity, friction coefficient, neural conductivity) is deliberate. The villagers don't understand these words (Ch15). Keep that contrast.

## 2. Quick Rules (hard continuity facts, don't break these)

**Esdel**
- Brown eyes (Ch26–27). When the Karunākṣa is active, the irises glow **red and the pupils pinch to vertical slits, like a fox's** (Ch23, 26–27, 42). Afterward they fade back to round and brown. **No blood from the eyes, no ruptured veins.**
- Knows only his name, "Esdel". Remembers nothing before the desert.
- His **arm** was severed above the elbow in the desert (Ch1). He woke in Edenridge with it whole (Ch5). Arwen insists he arrived with no such wound. Thumb-sized dark marks ran from elbow to wrist, pulsed, then faded to pale traces by Ch7 and were gone by Ch8.
- **Healing:** shallow wounds don't close right away, but they're gone by morning with no scar (Ch10–11). He healed seven arrow wounds within hours after using the Karunākṣa (Ch23). A knife graze under his jaw (Ch25) had no stated healing.
- Has scarred, working hands (Ch6) and a faint scar over his temple (Ch19–20).
- Wears **Lyora's mother's silver necklace** with a small flower charm (Ch21). It warms against his chest.
- Promised Lyora "I'll stay" in the garden (Ch19). **Broke it less than an hour later, by choice** (Ch20): the captain chose Lyora, she said yes as her mother once had, and Esdel stepped forward and went in her place. He persuaded the captain with the truth, told softly (the captain's daughter; Lyora's parents' story, which wasn't his to tell), and never looked at her. That night on the stairs she said "*You promised*," and he promised instead: "*I broke one promise to you tonight. I won't break another. I'll come back.*" He has kept that second promise (by staying away since Ch45) and carries the first as a wound (Ch22, 39, 43).
- **Esdel never lies.** Not to Lyora, not to anyone; it's why she always believed him. He may stay silent or withhold (e.g. the binding, Ch35), but he never says anything false, and never asks others to lie for him (Ch47–48). When he must say something hard, or do something that isn't right, he tells the truth **softly**, growing the listener's empathy until they understand (the captain, Ch20). Others may *call* something a lie (Eldric, Ch15), but on the page it's something he believed (Ch14).
- Fights by instinct, not memory (Ch18, 23). He can't use body-enhancement magic (Ch23).
- Titles and nicknames: "the man who never complains" (Edenridge), "the apprentice" (Edenridge, Ch7, 11), "Apothecary" (camp), "Hero" (Ch23), "the backwater genius" (Ch26).
- **Since Ch44 he is a declared traitor.** The decree names him "*the apothecary Esdel, of no family, formerly of the Third Logistic Company and the medical division of the North Ridge post, late of the village of Edenridge*." The bounty is **1,000 gold crowns, living or dead.** The decree has no description of his face. **Aldric withheld it on purpose** (Ch44, 47): he told the Ministry's limner he "barely remembers him," so Esdel will feel safe and, in time, head home to Edenridge, where the watchers wait. Esdel wondered about it once (Ch47–48) and let it go.
- **He can never go home** (Ch45). The name of the village is on the decree, and Aldric knows it (Ch36). He stays with Xueyao so the hunt runs *away* from Edenridge. This is how he keeps his promise to Lyora: by staying away.
- He burned every letter he wrote to Lyora (Ch36, 40). He keeps **one torn scrap of paper with a single word on it**, tucked behind the silver flower on the necklace (Ch40). The word is never revealed.
- Sent Lyora **a plain brown cord with three of his knots** through a south-bound peddler, with no message (Ch47–48).
- Never told Xueyao "the rest" about the tonic (that it *binds* the drinker to whoever spends the most time with them while it works; the pourer doesn't matter). He carries the note in his coat (Ch35).
- Used Halden's **blue vial** at the Greenwater ford (Ch43). He took a spear through the left side, and it closed on the second night (Ch45).
- **He learned his plants and poisons.** It isn't instinct. He learned from Arwen and Lyora as Arwen's **apprentice** in Edenridge (Ch5–16), and later from Halden (Ch35, 47). He writes in a "small, strange hand" that isn't like anyone else's ("as if a spider walked through the ink with great care," Ch6; Ch35).
- **The apprentice (Ch5–16):** every day starts at the shop at dawn (grind, sort, dry, sweep, labels). Village help and the bamboo happen **in the afternoons** ("The shop comes first. Mornings are hers. Afternoons, I'm yours," Ch7). He learned every shelf label overnight (Ch5). Lyora gave him a string-bound **notebook** of receipts (Ch6), and Arwen gave him a **slate** that he keeps under his bed for sums and puzzles at night (Ch6, 8). He measured his first dose alone, willow bark for a fever (Ch7), and helped treat Eldric's father's leg (Ch13–15). The farm is **Lyora's** to tend; Esdel went once in her place (Ch13) and once with Arwen (Ch14). Arwen called him "the best apprentice this house has had" (Ch16).
- **The grey-leaved plant (Ch9):** Lyora stopped his hand in a shaded hollow: "*We don't use that. Not ever.*" She gave no name. He drew it in his notebook and wrote only "*Not ever.*" He never asked Arwen why. It was greyveil (Ch35).
- **Personality on the page:** gentle humor that puts people at ease; he reads people and stops joking when it stops helping; he comforts; he asks "why" about everything; he makes friends easily. When a woman grows warm toward him he notices early and steps aside kindly (the mill-lane widow, Ch9). **Never explain why he is like this.**
- **He has never killed anyone in this Kingdom's service.** He spared the Count at the ford by choice: "I didn't want to be the kind of man who would" (Ch45). He did kill the enemy captain in Ch23.

**Lyora Ardynne Aurelisse**
- **Cries quietly, often, and hides it.** Her cover excuses: chamomile dust (Ch5), smoke (Ch6), "the sun" (Ch9), kettle steam (Ch10), onions (Ch16). Esdel never contradicts her and never lies; he says "Mm." See §11.
- Green eyes, long dark-brown hair (often braided or tied with cloth).
- Parents: father a healer, mother an apothecary. Soldiers took both to the frontlines and neither returned (Ch20). The necklace was her mother's, given the day she left (Ch21).
- Has never seen the ocean (Ch6). Hates tomato soup (Ch16). Loves cats. Made a potion with sugar instead of salt as a child, and glued a stray cat's fur at age five (Ch16).
- Her childhood crush was **Eldric** (Ch9). She gave up on him. It was Esdel she said "love" to (Ch17, "For making me love you this much").
- Almost strangled by a grieving soldier (Ch18).
- **Volunteered to go to the front** when the captain asked her (Ch20): "Yes. I'll come." Esdel took it from her. On the second step she **cried until she was empty**, saying everything ("It was supposed to be me," "Who will I leave the lamp for?") until he couldn't say a word and could only hold her. Then their eyes met, and he let her see everything he hadn't said. He was crying too.
- **Did not cry at the farewell** (Ch21): she had nothing left. She fastened the necklace on him herself ("you have to come back, to give it back to me"), then went home and **lit the lamp in broad daylight**. She didn't cry for weeks after (Ch22; the first tears come late, Ch24, "the steam") and whispers "*You promised*" to the lamp, then "*Be safe*." 
- **Jealous only once, and ashamed of it:** a stranger laughed with Esdel about a cart wheel (Ch13). She burned the tea twice (Ch13, 15). This is the "last time" teased in Ch16.
- Twisted her right ankle on the ridge in a summer storm; Esdel carried her down on his back (Ch14). It healed in about a week (Ch16).
- Said everything to Esdel on the stairs **except the word "love"** (Ch15). "Love" is first spoken in Ch17.
- Wrote him unsent, unsigned letters all winter and keeps them in her worktable drawer under the ledger (Ch34).
- Kept the lamp lit on the second step every night after he left, even through the winter (Ch34). Told Eldric "I'm waiting" (Ch38).
- Heard the decree read under the great tree (Ch46). That night she **doubted him**, beside the lamp: his name was listed after the Maiden's, and she wondered whether a man who forgets could remember one village girl. This is **fear of being forgotten, explicitly not jealousy** (she has been jealous only once, Ch13). She let the lamp burn down to a blue bead. At dawn the watchers sat down outside the inn and she understood the snare: he'll stay away *because* he remembers. She turned the lamp up brighter than ever, and it "never went that low again." The cord (Ch47–48) answers the doubt: "He had not forgotten the knot."
- **Received the three-knot cord** from a peddler in autumn of Year 2 and tied it to the base of the lamp on the stairs (Ch47–48). She knows it's from him. She has told no one, and Arwen never asked.

**The apothecary house (canon since Ch15)**
- Lyora's room is downstairs. Esdel's attic is up the narrow stairs. The **third stair used to creak**. Esdel fixed it after Ch15.
- Since Ch15: Lyora leaves **a lamp lit, turned low, at the foot of the stairs** every night. Two cups of tea in the morning, with Esdel's made the way *she* takes hers (honey, a sprig of mint, not too hot). He always eats the supper she leaves by the stairs.
- Lyora's **drying rack** against the south wall of the yard was given by Eldric but built by Esdel with his irrigation knots (Ch13). She knows.

**Arwen Lysandra Aurelisse**: Lyora's grandmother and the village apothecary. Silver hair (braided), soft green eyes. Calm and wise, with a quick wit in company (Ch16). She calls Esdel "child" and "my child." **Her hands have begun to tremble (Year 2)**. She can no longer strike a flint (Ch38). She gave up her own lamp oil in winter so the stair lamp could burn (Ch34). She keeps a **cedar box of old receipts**, some in her own younger hand and some in **Lyora's mother's** quick, slanting one ("that one Lyora's mother swore by, and I never did," Ch9). She doesn't linger on that hand. She knows why the house never uses the grey-leaved plant (Ch9, 35), though no one has asked her.

**Edenridge**
- A village between low hills, forest, and silver streams, at the edge of the desert. It has a river, a river shrine, the apothecary (ivy-draped, with the attic above), a market square, and a great tree in the square.
- **Village magic:** water-guiding charms used to irrigate the fields, but the last enchanter left "three winters ago" (Ch7). Esdel replaced them with **bamboo channels** (Ch7) and later a water wheel with Reyn (Ch7).
- **Seasons:** spring rain (Ch6) → early summer (Ch7) → midsummer feast (Ch8) → Festival of Wishing Threads, "once every summer" (Ch10–11) → hottest market day, harvest coming, summer storms (Ch13–15) → crisp wind (Ch16).
- **Festivals:** Devotional Gathering (monthly, river lanterns, Ch6). Festival of Flow (once a decade, Ch7). Festival of Wishing Threads (two nights: night one, threads of red for love / gold for fortune / blue for health / white for remembrance are hung across the streets under the crossing **twin stars**; night two, wish ribbons are tied to the great tree, Ch10–11).
- Villagers have no concept of "physics" (Ch8).
- **Year 2:** the bamboo channels survived a hard winter (Ch34). Eldric's father walks without a cane (Ch34). Eldric's mother is **Hessa** (Ch38).
- **After the decree (Ch46):** the scroll stays nailed to the great tree, and no one touches it. **Two men in grey** posing as wool buyers have lodged at the inn ever since, watching the lane to the apothecary. Reyn told the herald: "*We only knew an apothecary. And he was a good man.*"

**The Kingdom and the war**
- The Kingdom has a **King** (unnamed), a **Prince Ardbert** (heir), a **Prime Minister**, and a Supreme Commander.
- **Magic** exists: soldiers whisper incantations to "enhance their bodies" (Ch23), there are shields and "magic cannons" (Ch26), and a grieving soldier had "magic-fueled strength" (Ch18).
- The enemy wears dark armor with dull-crimson insignia (Ch23). The enemy also fields **Titans**, twenty-story horrors of pale, stretched flesh and rusted metal with blue-flame eyes (Ch26–27).
- Posts: the **Eastern Post** (Halden's medical division, Ch24–26), the **North Ridge post** (where Xueyao is stationed and where Esdel transfers, Ch26–27, 31). Ch31 names Esdel's unit the **Ninth Division**.
- **The capital:** white walls, terraced blue and green tile roofs, the palace on its hill, and a river running to the western harbor. There's the Hall of Clear Waters (banquets and decrees), the Hall of Remedies (the royal college of physicians), and the **north gate** at the top of the **Hundred Steps**. The Lián estate is in the eastern quarter by the river: the great gate, the east wing, the peach tree bound with iron, the pavilion by the pond, the east pavilion, the river gate, and the old stillroom behind the stables.
- **Greyveil root:** a grey-leaved paralytic used in small doses by field surgeons and in the royal college's "nervous exhaustion" receipt. In overdose it quiets the muscles "until the ones that breathe." Pinpoint pupils, blue lips.
- **Rewards (Ch44):** 5,000 gold crowns for Xueyao (living); 1,000 for each accomplice (living or dead). Helping her is treason. Lián soldiers still at the front were sent to penal companies, the Lián tombs sealed, and the Lián name banned from temples.

**The Karunākṣa**
- **What it is:** just a pair of **red eyes with fox-like slit pupils**. It takes in far more of the world than a mind normally handles (breath, tremor, tension, the arc of a falling arrow) and the brain processes it fast enough that time *seems* to freeze or slow. That's all it is.
- **The cost is the brain, not the eyes:** too much information. Light use gives a headache like needles in the skull. Heavy use gives nausea, ringing, trouble finding words, and slow thoughts for a day or more. Extreme overuse (Ch43) means days of confusion, lost names, and fever. **Recoverable every time**, but painful and annoying. No bleeding, no ruptured veins, no lasting damage so far.
- Under mortal pain, time freezes and he sees every body's tells and intentions (Ch23). Forcing it shut causes agony and a split-skull headache (Ch23).
- He later learned to trigger a lesser **Veil Form** with a slow, deliberate blink (Ch26–27): time slows and he reacts before thought.
- Xueyao says these are the eyes of the eradicated **House of Kālākṣa**, a line whose sight "the dragons themselves feared" (Ch27).
- **It is a battle eye, and not more than that.** It reads movement, fear, and intent: which man strikes next, where, how hard, the weak knee, the wrist that opens too wide. At full use it can predict even a wrist's motion. It does **not** see life spans, souls, or anything hidden inside a person. Its edge in a fight comes from pairing the sight with battle sense.
- **Meaning (Ch32):** *karuṇā* (mercy) + *akṣa* (eye) = "the eye of mercy." **The stories** say the Kālākṣa were healers who could see how long a heart had left; Xueyao calls that a legend that "grew in the telling." What her dragon showed her was a soldier's eye: people who "saw the blade before the hand that held it knew it would move." "Whatever mercy there was in it, they had to bring themselves." The dragons hated them; Xueyao's dragon showed her their image at fourteen. Who destroyed them is unknown.
- **Veil Form** (Ch42): he reads the bodies: held breath, jumping pulse in the throat, the weak knee, the half-breath before a man commits. Afterward comes the needles-in-the-skull headache, ringing, light and noise too loud, and a day or so of stupidity (lost words, slow thoughts). **Never his eyes bleeding.**
- **Full form** (Ch43, triggered by a mortal wound plus Halden's vial): the world *stops* and turns red. Overuse hits the brain: afterward he lost words and names for **two days** and had a three-day fever. The eyes stayed red a day (Ch45). Recoverable, painful, and annoying.
- **Aldric at the ford** (Ch43, 45): of thirty men, only Aldric was **not afraid**: his breathing and the pulse in his throat were as calm as a man at supper under a face blazing with joy, the "fever-brightness" Esdel has seen on the dying. The eyes saw nothing *inside* him. Esdel: "if I kill him, I'll never know what put that look there."

**Lián Xueyao**: the Maiden of the Heavenly Dragon.
- Golden eyes, long black hair, pale skin. Wears ivory-and-gold battle attire and, later, a dark general's uniform.
- She commands the **Imperial Dragon**, a golden serpentine beast. Their pact: she commands, it obeys, but it **feeds on souls**, and prisoners are brought to its altar. It first spoke to her at **fourteen** (Ch26–27).
- Rose from knight to **General**. She has been **three years** at the front and hasn't been home since (Ch31).
- Her attendant is **Changli**, a childhood friend of the same age and her lady's maid. She is beautiful, cunning, and uses charm as a tool (Ch31).
- She met Esdel at the North Ridge roadside the night the Titan fell, saw the Karunākṣa, and asked "Who are you?" She asked three times (Ch27, 32, 47). The third time he answered: "*Esdel. For now, I think it's enough.*"
- About **23** in Year 2 (fourteen at the pact plus nine years). Changli has been her friend "since we were six," seventeen years. Her mother called her **"Yaoyao."** She has her mother's dry, quick voice.
- The **Heavenly Dragon is bound to the Lián blood**, not to her alone. When she dies it passes to the next of the blood, which is **Yueming** (Ch37). This is why Aldric wanted both children.
- **Since Ch44: a traitor**, stripped of rank and name, worth 5,000 crowns. She has **cut her hair short** and wears her **white mourning robe** under a dark cloak (Ch47). She is hiding in the north-eastern borderlands.
- She **never summoned the dragon** in this arc. Tempted under the peach tree (Ch39), she chose her mother's wish (keep Yueming safe) over vengeance.
- She feels something for Esdel that she never says. He is faithful to Lyora, and she knows Lyora's name (Ch39). No romance.
- Weeps only in silence, "the way soldiers learn to" (Ch33, 39).
